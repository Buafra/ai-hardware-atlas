# AI Hardware Atlas

Cipher Lacuna ("Decoding the gaps in AI knowledge"): public NVIDIA / AMD comparison, AI news and UAE AI, as one static bilingual page, plus a one-page PDF and an official-source checker. AI Hardware Atlas is the name of the hardware area.

## Current scope

- Four-pillar hub. `#home` is a short hero plus four equal pillars (Hardware, AI news, UAE AI, Learn AI; two by two on wide screens, one column on phones and tablets), each a mini-dashboard with stats computed from the data and an "Open …" button. Inner views: `#hardware` (the full tool below), `#news`, `#uae` and `#contact`, each with a top nav, breadcrumb and "Back to overview". Unknown hashes open `#home`. Deep links such as `#hardware/level/Personal`, `#news/uae` or `#contact/about` are applied and then reduced to the view; a product or a key fact (`#hardware/p/<id>`, `#uae/f/<id>`, also reached from the plain anchors `#p-<id>` and `#fact-<id>`) keeps its deep link in the address, so the address can be shared, until the next navigation or until the product leaves the cards (another filter, the table or the timeline). Filters stay in the query string next to the hash, so Copy link shares both. Without JavaScript all views render one after another.
- Featured products and the UAE highlight (one key fact) on `#home` are chosen in `data/site.json`: `featured_products` (three) and `uae_highlights` (only the first id is used). Unknown ids are skipped and gaps filled from the data.
- Brand: Cipher Lacuna, with the owner's logo (`web/brand/logo.svg`) before the wordmark and the tagline under it in English or Arabic (the name stays Latin). Logo and favicon are sanitised and embedded as images, so they cannot run script; the PNG icons and the 1200 x 630 share preview (`web/brand/og.png`, made by `python scripts/make_og.py`) are copied to `dist/brand/`. Open Graph and X card tags point to https://cipherlacuna.ae/. See `web/brand/README.md`.
- GPU, system and rack entries, kept in `data/catalog.json`.
- Search and filters; numeric memory, bandwidth, power and launch-price sorting; chronological date-window sorting.
- Separate announcement and availability/target dates; memory scope on every entry.
- Specs where officially published: memory bandwidth, headline AI compute (precision and sparsity as stated), interconnect, form factor, cooling and launch MSRP. Unknown values show "Not listed".
- Three views: cards, a sortable table and a timeline of announcement vs availability.
- Side-by-side comparison of up to 4 products.
- "What can it run?" estimator: model size x precision + "extra memory for chat length and software" (default 20%, `extra` in the URL), with fit badges and a fits-only filter.
- The hardware search also lists matching AI models (same matching and ranking as the Android app: `searchModels` / `modelSuggestions` in `web/app.js`, from 2 characters): an "AI models (N)" card above the products, open with the first 3 (and "Show all N models") when no hardware matches, a title line with "Show models" when hardware matches too. Picking a model clears the search and sets it in the estimator. Tests: `tests/test-sort.cjs`, `tests/e2e_model_search.py`.
- Shareable links: search, filters, sort, view, comparison and estimator are kept in the URL; the language is the address (`/` or `/ar/`).
- English / Arabic (RTL) interface, light / dark / auto theme, printable table. Every page exists in both languages: `index.html`, `learn.html` and `qahwa.html` in English and `ar/index.html`, `ar/learn.html` and `ar/qahwa.html` in Arabic, each served already in its language (see "Arabic pages, search basics and feeds" below).
- Official product photos (NVIDIA / AMD, credited and linked), stored as small WebP files in `images/` (`python scripts/images.py`).
- Approximate current prices in USD and AED: dated retail listings or reported estimates, with sources (`price` in the catalog). Without a UAE listing, AED is converted at the 3.6725 peg and labelled. The review issue flags a price on the day it is 30 days old (`STALE_PRICE_DAYS` in `scripts/review_issue.py`), once, with a new comment.
- AI news in English and Arabic (full lists grouped by day in UAE time, Global / UAE filter), refreshed with every scheduled run from 41 vetted sources (`data/news-sources.json`, `scripts/news.py`; the source list itself is not shown on the page). The latest two are global: Alibaba's official Qwen blog (a primary source) and The Decoder (a news outlet). Like every global source they cannot carry stories that mention the UAE or the GCC states (rule M1 below). Each headline card shows the headline as text, the summary (in full in the AI news and UAE AI lists, about three lines on the overview), a small label saying who wrote it, then a small "Source: <publisher> ↗" link to the original article and the date. The "Updated <date> · twice a day" line appears once on the site, at the top of the AI news view. Stored per item: headline, link, source, date and an excerpt of the publisher's own description (at most 280 characters, publisher boilerplate, wire datelines and invisible characters removed, never cut after an abbreviation or inside a quotation, labelled "From the publisher"). The excerpt comes from the feed or, when the feed only repeats the headline or gives nothing, from the description in the article page's own `<meta>` tags. Links and page reads must stay on the publisher's domains (redirects are checked before they are followed) and page reads respect the site's robots.txt. Per-source settings in `news-sources.json`: `feed_excerpt`, `excerpt_full_sentences`, `prefer_ai_summary`, `drop_titles`, `drop_links` (e.g. sponsored or partner links), `drop_authors` (paid content that shares the news links, matched against the feed's author and categories: Khaleej Times' "Partner Content" / "KT Engage", MIT Technology Review Insights), `weak_ai_terms` (The Register: "datacenter" alone does not make an AI headline), `raw_limit`, `timeout` (45 s for the slow Dubai Media Office feeds and the Qwen list), `max_bytes` (largest feed accepted, 8 MB by default; 24 MB for the Qwen list, which carries every post in full), `page_stop` / `page_drop` (page furniture left out of the text sent for AI summaries; headings such as "Related content" or "Most popular" end it for every source), `read_pages`, `link_prefix` (one language of a mixed feed) and `feed_type` (see the top of `scripts/news.py`). Each feed request asks for its own type (RSS, XML for sitemaps, HTML for the Anthropic page, JSON for the Qwen list). The newest 80 entries of a feed are read, or 600 of a news sitemap, whose publishers list hundreds of stories a day. One story is kept once per language: the same link from two sources, or headlines that share most of their words within 48 hours (a wire story carried by several outlets), keep the copy from an official or primary source first, then one with an excerpt or summary. `feed_type` says how the one request per source per run is read: `rss` (the default: RSS 2.0, RSS 1.0 or Atom); `news_sitemap`, a Google News sitemap (`<news:title>`, `<news:publication_date>`) for publishers with no RSS (WAM, Al Bayan, Sharjah24, The Decoder), with excerpts taken from the article page; `qwen_articles`, the JSON article list behind qwen.ai/research (it has no feed; title, date and the post's introduction, cut back to whole sentences; the article pages are an app shell, so `read_pages` is off); and `anthropic_listing`, anthropic.com's listing page (it has no feed), read from the post list embedded in the page (title, summary, publish time) or else its rendered links, with `listing_directory` `news` (the default) or `research`. Sources that share a feed URL are fetched once per run. Each run keeps the newest 200 items of the last 14 days plus up to 3 of each source's newest that this cap would leave out, so the quieter official feeds are not crowded out by busy ones.
- WAM (Emirates News Agency): its public news sitemap gives headlines only, and its article pages are filled in by script, so they can't be read. The owner registered for WAM's RSS (Oct 2026), whose items carry the whole article. `text_feeds_env` in `news-sources.json` names the GitHub secrets `WAM_RSS_EN` and `WAM_RSS_AR`, each holding WAM's RSS feed URLs (one per line: science and technology, UAE news, latest, economy, world, culture). The URLs hold the owner's access code, so they live only in those secrets, and a failed feed is counted, never printed. Each feed lists its newest 20 stories, so a sitemap headline gets the text (excerpt, UAE check, AI summary) when it is still in one of them; the rest stay headlines only. Without the secrets nothing changes. To renew them, sign in at wam.ae/en/rss and wam.ae/ar/rss and replace the secrets' values.
- News images (owner-approved hybrid, Sep 2026): every headline card has an image, beside the text on wide screens, a 96x72 thumbnail beside the title on phones and 80x60 on the overview's pillars. Stories from official company sources (`kind` "primary" with an `image_hosts` allow-list in `news-sources.json`: NVIDIA, OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, Google DeepMind, Google Research, Hugging Face, Microsoft; AWS and MIT News use topic images: AWS's images are text templates, and MIT credits some of its photos to stock agencies) show the company's own image from its post (the feed's image, else the article page's `og:image` / `twitter:image`), with a credit line "Image: <company>" / «الصورة: <company>»; every other story shows the site's own topic image (`web/news-img/<topic>.svg`, 15 topics, picked by the keyword rules in `scripts/topics.py`). UAE/GCC official sources (WAM, Dubai Media Office, Sharjah24, anything marked `official_region`) always get the UAE topic image, never their photos; so does any company story whose headline, translation or excerpt names a country, a government or a national event (English and Arabic keywords, GCC names first: such photos can show a flag or a head of state). News-agency photos are never used: only hosts on the source's own allow-list (its domains, or a path on a shared CDN such as `images.ctfassets.net/<OpenAI's space>/`); `image_skip` drops generic logo cards (Google Research's preview card), a picture a source uses for several stories counts as generic too, and `image_page_hosts` lets an article page redirect to another of the company's own domains for this lookup only (DeepMind posts move to blog.google). `image_page_paths` limits images to the company's own posts (Hugging Face: `/blog/<slug>`, not a partner's `/blog/<org>/<slug>`). AMD is left out: its press releases carry one generic social image on its investor-relations provider's shared CDN. MIT News is left out: its pictures are often stock or third-party ("Photo: iStock", "courtesy of ...") and licensed non-commercial only. `scripts/news_images.py` (called by `build.py`, never failing it) downloads each image once (image/* only, at most 5 MB), converts it to WebP (at most 480 px wide and 40 KB) and serves it as `images/news/<id>.webp`, so the page loads nothing from other hosts. `.cache/news-img/` (git-ignored, restored before the build and saved right after it, even if a later step fails, by the workflow's `actions/cache/restore` and `actions/cache/save` steps) holds the converted images and an index (id, image and page URL, fetched-at); a story with no usable image is remembered, one that hit a network error, an unreadable robots.txt or a 401/403/429 bot wall is tried again up to 3 times in a row, then once a day. The app's `news.json` gets `topic` and `image` (site-relative) per headline, and `image_credit` for a company image. Tests: `tests/test_topics.py`, `tests/test_news_images.py` (no network), `tests/test_news_images_redteam.py` (a local test server only).
- With `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY` (a repository secret; not set locally, and the tests mock the SDK): recent English headlines get an Arabic translation (not those already judged not mainly about AI; the summary step runs first), marked «ترجمة بالذكاء الاصطناعي» ("AI translation"), and up to 80 items per run (headlines with no useful excerpt first, UAE first, newest first) get a summary of 3–5 sentences, about 80–120 words, in English and Arabic (what happened, who is involved, when and where, and why it matters, only as the text states it; `claude-opus-5`, structured output, 5 items per request, over-long answers trimmed to whole sentences at 1,000 characters) plus an `ai_focus` verdict. Items judged not mainly about AI (a weekend digest, a lifestyle piece that only lists AI) stay in `data/news.json` but are left out of every list; items without a verdict follow the keyword rule. Each summary records its `summary_version`; raising `SUMMARY_VERSION` in `scripts/news.py` has older summaries rewritten (they stay until the new one succeeds, and earlier failed tries don't count). Summaries are written from the article page when it can be read on the publisher's own domain (15 s, 1.5 MB, 6,000 characters of paragraph text) or else from the headline and excerpt. They are labelled "AI summary" / «ملخص بالذكاء الاصطناعي», kept across runs, and API errors skip a batch without failing the run. Only a Claude request counts as one of a story's two summary tries: a story with nothing to send (no readable page text and no excerpt, e.g. a WAM story that is not, or not yet, in WAM's text feed) is not asked and keeps waiting until it leaves the 14-day window; its page is read for this at most twice (`summary_page_misses`), so a page that refuses the bot is not fetched on every run, while text from WAM's feed is still used whenever it arrives. Tries the older code used up this way, without a request, are given back once, in code, by the first run under the new rule (recorded as `ai.summary_tries_rule` in `data/news.json`). What the AI steps did, or why they did nothing (no key, API errors, refusals), is recorded in `data/news.json` under `ai` (not shown on the site). The English page shows the English summary or an English excerpt; the Arabic page shows the Arabic summary or an Arabic excerpt.
- Content policy for the UAE and the GCC states (Saudi Arabia, Qatar, Kuwait, Bahrain, Oman), approved by the owner and applied to the site and the Android app (`scripts/policy.py`). Nothing is shown that criticises or casts in a negative light these countries, their emirates and regions, rulers, ruling families, officials, governments, armed forces, national symbols or state-linked entities (P1); that covers them in a negative or controversial political frame such as human-rights, surveillance, espionage, conflict, sanctions or export-control disputes (P2); that could harm national unity, public order or relations between states, or offends Islam or any religion (P3); or that spreads rumours about officials or institutions (P4). When in doubt, the story is left out, never rewritten. How the news step applies it (`scripts/news.py`):
  - M1: a story that mentions the region (English or Arabic, any spelling the detector knows: places, nationalities, leaders, ruling families, state entities) is kept only from a regional outlet: the UAE newsrooms (`"region": "uae"`, including the official Dubai Media Office, WAM and Sharjah24 feeds) and the GCC outlets marked `"regional_outlet": true` (Sky News Arabia, Independent Arabia). Another official UAE/GCC source can be marked `"official_region": true`. Global vendor newsrooms (NVIDIA, AMD, OpenAI, Anthropic ...) are not official sources of the region.
  - M2: with `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY`, every item gets an AI verdict (`policy_ok`, `policy_version`, `policy_hash`) before it is shown; a changed headline, translation, excerpt or summary needs a new one. Contradictory answers count as a fail; a story the model refuses or cannot judge twice in a run is removed. Without a key, or when the API fails, stories that mention the region stay hidden (fail closed).
  - M4: the repository is public, so nothing unverified that could still fail is written to `data/news.json`: a regional story only with a passing verdict and, with the key set, a new story only once it has one. A failing story is removed entirely; `data/news-blocked.json` keeps only one-way hashes (of its canonical URL, and of its source and headline) for 60 days so it is not collected again, even with a tracking query. Logs, `news.json` and the review issue show counts only. With the `TG_BOT_TOKEN` and `TG_CHAT_ID` secrets set, titles and reasons go privately to the owner on Telegram.
  - M5: `scripts/learn.py` checks the fixed content (Learn AI, `data/uae.json`, `data/about.json` and the hardware notes in `data/catalog.json`) and the build stops if a text field pairs a UAE/GCC name with conflict, attack, sanctions, criticism, detention, surveillance or similar wording.
  Raise `POLICY_VERSION` in `scripts/policy.py` whenever the policy text changes: every story is checked again.

- UAE AI section: the latest AI headlines about the UAE first (a story counts as UAE news only when its headline, excerpt or summary names a UAE place, ruler or entity, whoever publishes it; publishers' own names such as Sharjah24 or «الإمارات اليوم» don't count — `policy.mentions_uae`), then the sourced key-fact cards (`data/uae.json`, reviewed by hand, each with its "as of" date). - News search (owner request, Oct 2026): a "Search news…" / «ابحث في الأخبار…» field in the side panel of AI news (above the Region chips) and of UAE AI (headlines only; the key facts are never filtered). It searches what each card shows in the page language: the headline (the AI translation on the Arabic list), the summary shown and the publisher's name, never labels, dates or tags. Same rules as the Android app (`normText` / `searchWords` / `matchesWords` in `web/app.js`; the shared cases in `tests/test-sort.cjs` match the app's `NewsSearchTest.kt`): case, accents, Arabic diacritics and tatweel are ignored, أ/إ/آ/ٱ count as ا, ة as ه, ى as ي, ؤ as و, ئ as ي, ک and گ as ك, ڤ as ف, پ as ب, چ as ج, Arabic-Indic digits as 0–9, punctuation as a space; every word must appear, also inside a longer word or across punctuation ("gpt5" finds "GPT-5"). It starts at 2 characters (100 at most). With a region chosen, AI news shows the headlines matching both, and the chips count the matches. "Show more" pages through the matches; a search without results says so (never repeating the query, since a link can set it) with a "Clear search" / «مسح البحث» button, in the count line's place as in the app (no "No results" line above it; the count's live region holds the message, so a screen reader hears it once). The (x) empties the field without taking the focus from it; after a tap on "Clear search" the focus goes to the list's heading, so the phone keyboard stays closed. The queries live in the URL (`?nq=` for AI news, `?uq=` for UAE AI; without a hash they open their view), so a reload keeps them and Back or Forward restores the search of that history entry (the URL wins, as for the region); the nav links, a `#news/global` link, the language switch and the hardware Reset keep both. Tests: `tests/test-sort.cjs`, `tests/test_build.py` (`NewsSearchMarkupTests`), `tests/e2e_news_search.py` (browser; `--shots DIR` saves the owner's screenshots).
- Topic chips in AI news (owner request, Oct 2026): beside the search, "Topic" / «الموضوع» chips: All, then each topic that has headlines (`scripts/topics.py`, the same rule as the topic images, in its order; names in both languages, "Other AI news" for the stories no other topic took), each with its count in the page language. - News archive (owner request, Oct 2026): every story that leaves `data/news.json` (its feed no longer lists it, or the 14-day window or the item cap leaves it out) is kept in `data/news-archive/YYYY-MM.json` (`scripts/news_archive.py`, month of publication in UAE time): `{"month", "policy_version", "items"}`, each item exactly `id, source, lang, uae, topic, published, title, title_ar, url, summary_en, summary_ar, excerpt` (null when the story has none; the summaries are the AI summaries the site showed, `title_ar` the AI translation). Only stories with a passing content-policy verdict at the current version for their current texts, not judged "not mainly about AI", from a followed source; never a story removed under the policy or a copy of a story kept from another source. One entry per id and per link, written only when something changed (idempotent); a month file that cannot be read is left untouched for a person to repair. A story that comes back into the window (same id or link) gets its archived AI summary and Arabic headline again, so Claude is not paid twice; its content check runs again like any story entering the window. A story on the block list (`data/news-blocked.json`, `scripts/blocklist.py`) is taken out of the newest month files on every run (`news_archive.drop`): one that comes back, fails its new check and is blocked leaves the archive too. The build shows archived stories only under the same rules as the list (rule M1, and a story that mentions the UAE or the GCC only from a file checked under the current `POLICY_VERSION`; fail closed after a policy change), never a story on the block list (a second guard) and never a story still in the list. The first run that archives records the day the archive began in `data/news-archive/started.json` (UAE time). Counts are recorded in `data/news.json` under `archive`.

Chat-only models (`runnable` false, no downloadable weights, such as Falcon-H1-Arabic) are listed as "Chat only — no download" and never fitted to hardware. Tests: `tests/test_models_extra.py`, `tests/test-sort.cjs`.
- My hardware: a visitor can mark one product as "My hardware" (the ☆ button on its card, or the menu in the estimator). It is kept in this browser only (`localStorage` `atlas-myhw`, through the same try/catch wrapper as the theme; the page works the same without it, and it is never in the URL). The card then shows a "My hardware" tag, and the estimator keeps it selected after a reload, says whether the chosen model fits it and lists the models that fit it at the chosen precision and extra memory, largest first (`modelsThatFit` in `web/app.js`, the same rule as the fit badges; the "Arabic models" chip applies too). Clear it with the menu, the Clear button or the card button.
- Official benchmarks (MLPerf): a product that appears in an MLPerf round under its exact MLCommons accelerator or system name has `benchmarks` in `data/catalog.json`: `{label, url, round, date}`, one per round (Inference v6.1 and v6.0, Training v6.0, v5.1 and v5.0), linking to the round's official results record. Links only: no scores or other figures are copied. `build.validate` accepts only those four keys, https links on `mlcommons.org` or `github.com/mlcommons/`, an "MLPerf …" round and a real date. The product's "Notes and official sources" shows them as "Official benchmarks (MLPerf)", newest round first; the app gets them through `catalog.json`. Tests: `tests/test_benchmarks.py`.
- Embedded fonts and one-page A3 PDF, generated from the same catalog.
- Scheduled workflow: twice a day, every 12 hours: 02:07 and 14:07 Dubai time (22:07 and 10:07 UTC; GitHub started the earlier 03:15 / 15:15 UTC slots 3.4 to 7.5 hours late). Each scheduled run first logs how late it started (`scripts/run_timing.py`: the cron slot that fired, the actual start and the delay in minutes, the newest 60 runs in `data/run-timing.json`, saved with the refreshed data, not published). Only these runs call Claude (news summaries, Arabic headlines, the content-policy check, hardware drafts), each AI step as one Message Batch at half the price of single requests (`NEWS_BATCH=1`). A summary batch still running after 20 minutes is never cancelled: it is recorded in `data/news-batches.json` (batch and item ids only) and a later run collects its answers once it has ended (Anthropic ends every batch within 24 hours; requests it never answered expire unbilled). Its stories show the publisher's excerpt meanwhile and are not asked again until 25 hours after the batch was created; an entry the API no longer knows, or that still cannot be read after 7 days, is given up. The entry leaves the file only after `news.json` holds the answers (not while a story it answered is held back). Headlines and the content check are always asked directly, not in a batch: both must finish within the run, and from 30 Sep to 2 Oct 2026 every batch took more than 20 minutes, so no story could be approved while the content check was batched. A push, a manual run or an Android release only rebuilds the site, unless the manual run ticks "ai". Each run records the tokens it used and an estimate of their cost in `data/news.json` under `ai.usage`, and prints them in the workflow log; `news.py` and `draft.py` also add their estimates to a monthly ledger, `data/ai-cost.json` (month -> `usd`, `runs`; `scripts/ai_cost.py`; not published), shown in the review issue's status comment. A collected batch kept for a story that was held back is read again by later runs for free; its tokens count only on the first read (the entry is marked `counted` in `data/news-batches.json`). On these runs the tests also run before the paid steps (a failing test stops the run before Claude is paid for, so no paid answers are thrown away) and again after the data changed, before anything is saved or published; the Android APK cache is restored before the first test, so the real release's certificate test runs in CI instead of being skipped.

## Publish

1. Create a public repository called `ai-hardware-atlas` and push this directory to `main`.
2. Repository Settings > Pages > Source: GitHub Actions.
   Settings > Actions > General > Workflow permissions: enable **Allow GitHub Actions to create and approve pull requests** (for AI drafts).
   Optional: add an `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY` repository secret to turn on AI drafts.
3. Run **Update hardware and publish** under Actions (manual run also checks sources).
4. Confirm the deployment succeeds; use the URL reported by the deployment job.

No API key is needed for the current source checker. GitHub's scheduled jobs can be delayed. GitHub may disable scheduled workflows in an inactive public repository after 60 days; inspect Actions if checks stop.

## Android app releases

The website serves only the newest release; a new release replaces the file at the same address. To publish a version:

1. Build and sign it (the app's own repository; the owner signs with the Cipher Lacuna key). Every version needs a higher `versionCode`.
2. Publish it as a release of this repository, tag `android-v<versionName>`, the APK uploaded as `Cipher-Lacuna.apk`:

   ```bash
   cp Cipher-Lacuna-1.1.0.apk upload/Cipher-Lacuna.apk
   gh release create android-v1.1.0 upload/Cipher-Lacuna.apk -R Buafra/ai-hardware-atlas --title "Cipher Lacuna for Android 1.1.0" --notes "..."
   ```

   The file must be named exactly `Cipher-Lacuna.apk` on disk: GitHub keeps the file's own name, and `gh`'s `path#text` only sets a display label (a release of 1.1.0 uploaded as `Cipher-Lacuna-1.1.0.apk#Cipher-Lacuna.apk` was skipped by the site until the file was uploaded again under the right name). Check with `gh release view android-vX.Y.Z --json assets --jq '.assets[].name'`.
3. The release starts **Update hardware and publish** on main (`.github/workflows/android-release.yml` dispatches it: the site deploys from main only). `scripts/android_app.py` picks the highest `android-vX.Y.Z` release (not drafts or pre-releases) and downloads its APK. It checks the file before the site serves it:
   - the size and SHA-256 match GitHub's record;
   - it is a ZIP holding `AndroidManifest.xml`;
   - its v2/v3 signature block carries the Cipher Lacuna certificate (SHA-256 `1d3d4f1b…b6f912`). A debug build, an unsigned build or another key is refused, and the previous version stays on the site.

Old releases stay on GitHub as history; the site never links them. Deleting every Android release removes the download card on the next run. The APK is cached between runs (`.cache/android`, actions/cache), so a failed GitHub request keeps the current download. Tests: `tests/test_android_app.py`, and `tests/e2e_android.py` in a browser.

## What updates automatically

The workflow fetches official NVIDIA / AMD pages, discovers relevant official headlines, stores check health, and regenerates the website and PDF. Narrow, product-title-checked parsers update explicitly labeled specification fields configured in `data/refresh-rules.json`. Missing, conflicting or blocked source responses preserve existing data. A source that fails keeps a short, safe reason in `data/refresh-report.json` (`errors`: the HTTP status, the network error or the check that refused the page; no addresses). NVIDIA Marketplace pages (`marketplace.nvidia.com`) are drawn by scripts in the browser, so a plain request gets no text: they stay as sources in the catalog but are not fetched, and the report lists them under `manual_checks` (shown in the review issue) to check by hand.

Parsers exist for 19 products (`data/refresh-rules.json`); each was checked against the live official page. Pages that serve several variants only have the unambiguous fields configured.

The review issue (`scripts/review_issue.py`, one open GitHub issue labelled `source-review`) has ONE status comment that every run edits (found by a hidden marker and written by github-actions[bot]; editing a comment sends no email): source health, the month's estimated AI cost, the AI news steps, how late scheduled runs start, AI drafts, ageing prices, the official pages that changed and the failing sources with their reasons. A new comment, and so an email, is posted only when the owner must act, once per event: a verified specification change, a newly failing source (failed in two checks in a row; `failures` in `data/source-state.json`), AI drafts waiting (a new draft, or the pull request could not be opened), a price reaching the age limit, or old announcements settled without a draft (below). What was already announced is a hidden list at the end of the status comment; a comment by anyone else is never trusted. The alert is posted before the status comment is written and only alerts that were posted join that list, so if posting fails the next run posts them again (a spec change or a newly failing source, which a later run does not see again, is carried in a second hidden list until it is posted). With no open issue, one is opened only when the owner must act.

**AI drafts (optional).** With an `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY` secret, up to 3 newly discovered official headlines per run are sent to Claude (`claude-sonnet-5-5`, structured output). A draft is kept only if every evidence quote appears verbatim on the official page, it is a new product, and dates are well formed. Drafts go to `data/drafts/` in an `ai-drafts` pull request (opened by `peter-evans/create-pull-request`, pinned to the commit of v8.1.1); they never enter the catalog by themselves. An announcement counts as done only once its draft is in that pull request: until then the draft waits in `data/draft-state.json` ("pending", with the whole record), every run writes it to `data/drafts/` again without asking Claude again, and the pull request is tried again; `python scripts/draft.py --pr-result` records the outcome of the pull-request step before the data is saved, and a failed step is reported in the review issue (usually the repository setting below is off). Drafts of a pull request that is still open are written again too, so a later run adds to it instead of emptying it; once the product is in the catalog or the pull request is merged or closed, the draft leaves the state. The pull-request step runs only on a run where `draft.py` just ran without failing, so a run without it (a push, a manual run without "ai", an Android release) never rebuilds `ai-drafts` without the open drafts. Announcements that the old code marked "drafted" before pull requests were tracked (no pull request was opened and their drafts were not kept) stay settled and are not drafted again on their own, which would pay Claude again, mostly for products already in the catalog under another name; the run that settles them lists them once in the review issue. To draft one again (one Claude request): `python scripts/draft.py --redraft URL`, then commit `data/draft-state.json`. After checking a draft against the source:

```bash
python scripts/draft.py --promote data/drafts/<id>.json
```

Availability is never promoted to “released” just because a target date passed.

The on-page source-check time means a fetch pass ran. The per-product content review date is separate. The original catalog was reviewed 23 September 2026, with targeted corrections on 26 September. Some broad release windows remain vendor targets.

## Run locally

```bash
python -m venv .venv                    # once; then use the project's own Python for everything below
.venv/Scripts/python -m pip install -r requirements.txt   # on macOS/Linux: .venv/bin/python
.venv/Scripts/python -m unittest discover -s tests
node tests/test-sort.cjs
.venv/Scripts/python scripts/refresh.py
.venv/Scripts/python scripts/build.py                 # add --standalone ../../AI_Hardware_Atlas_2026.html to refresh the offline copy

```

Open http://localhost:8080. `dist/index.html` is self-contained and can also be opened directly. Keep the generated PDF next to it for the download button.

Learn AI is a separate static page, `learn.html`, rendered by `scripts/learn.py` from `data/learn/concepts.json` and `data/learn/stacks.json` (validated first; a data error fails the build). `scripts/build.py` writes it into `dist/` beside `index.html`, and with `--standalone` also beside the offline copy, with its links back pointing to that file. The overview links to it from the header, the footer and its fourth pillar (counts and picks come from the same data; `load_all()` validates it before anything is written, so a data error leaves `dist/` as it was). The Arabic overview (`ar/index.html`) links to the Arabic copy beside it (`ar/learn.html`), and back, so the links need no language parameter; only an English visit while Arabic is the saved choice (`?lang=en`, which saves nothing) adds `?lang=en` to them, or the English pages would send the visitor to their Arabic copies. Its browser test: `.venv/Scripts/python tests/e2e_learn.py http://localhost:8080/`.

Learn AI has four tabs: Concepts, AI stacks, **Reports** and **Courses** (below). Reports («التقارير», `#reports`, deep links `#report/<id>`). Reports lists free AI reports from consultancies, research bodies and UAE/GCC sources (`data/learn/reports.json`, newest first), each summarised in our own words: title, publisher and publication date as the publisher states it, region and access badges ("Free" or "Free, registration required"), 3–5 key findings, one "why it matters" line and a link to the publisher's official page (new tab, `rel="noopener noreferrer"`). Filters: source (Consultancies, Research & global bodies, UAE & GCC; a report about the UAE or the GCC goes under UAE & GCC), topic and year, plus the page's search. Copyright rules, checked by `scripts/learn.py` (a failure stops the build): links only to the publisher's own https domains (`REPORT_ORGS` / `REPORT_PUBLISHERS` allow-list; add a publisher there first), no re-hosted copies, no images, logos or charts (only the listed fields are accepted), findings and "why it matters" at most 320 characters each, and quoted phrases in the English text at most six words. Paid or client-only research is not listed. The content policy guard covers every report field. How each report was checked (pages, figures, wording changes) is kept privately outside this repository, not in `reports.json`.

The **Courses** tab («الدورات», `#courses`, deep links `#course/<id>`), "Free official AI courses in the UAE" / «دورات رسمية مجانية في الذكاء الاصطناعي في الإمارات», lists free AI courses from UAE government bodies and universities (`data/learn/courses.json`, in the file's order): who it is for, the languages, the format, the organiser, the cost line, how to join, any AI episodes, the official page (and its Arabic page) and the date it was checked. A course is listed only when its organiser's own official page says it is free (`free_source`); paid courses are never listed. Rules, checked by `scripts/learn.py` (`course_problems`; a failure stops the build): organisers on the `COURSE_ORGS` allow-list, every link https on that organiser's own domains, a "Free" cost line in English and «مجاني» in Arabic, known languages, formats and statuses, a real date that is not in the future, and the content policy guard. `learn.json` for the app carries them as `courses`. Tests: `tests/test_courses.py`.

Canonical tags on `/` (`index.html` is the same page) and `/learn.html`. JSON-LD only from facts the site already shows: WebSite + Organization on the overview (no contact address), an ItemList of LearningResource on Learn AI (concepts and stacks) and on the lessons page (published lessons, with their Instagram posts), and a SoftwareApplication on `/apk/` from `download/latest.json` (`android_app.latest()`; none without a checked APK). `favicon.ico` (16/32/48 px from `web/brand/icon-512.png`) and `site.webmanifest` from the brand icons with `"display": "browser"`: not installable, so Chrome on Android never offers to install the website instead of the APK; the iPhone Home Screen keeps its own tags (`apple-touch-icon`, `apple-mobile-web-app-*`). Learn AI's description is about 155 characters. `404.html` is a branded page in both languages with links to `/` and `/ar/` (root-relative, since GitHub Pages serves it at any depth; noindex). The weekly pages, `/apk/` and `404.html` have a light/dark switch (`scripts/theme_toggle.py`): Auto, Light, Dark, saved under the overview's key (`atlas-theme`), so a choice made on any page holds on all of them; tests in `tests/test_theme_toggle.py`.

- Tests: `tests/test_lang_pages.py` (the address rule run in node, translations read the way node reads them, links), `tests/test_seo.py` (a whole build in a temporary folder: every link of every page resolves, the sitemap, the standalone copy's Arabic twin), `tests/test_feeds.py`, `tests/test-sort.cjs`, and the browser tests above.

## Data integrity

- One source of truth for website, CSV and PDF.
- Source URLs restricted to official HTTPS vendor domains, including redirects.
- No secrets in HTML or JSON.
- Source fetch failures never delete products or replace values with zero.
- A failed build never reaches the deploy job.
- Changes are committed for rollback; source reports are retained as workflow artifacts.
- Human-edited fields are not sent to or executed by a model.
